Floorplan Studio — User Guide

Source One Events · internal manual

1What this system is

Floorplan Studio is Source One's own tool for turning a venue into a sellable, buildable floor plan — and for letting each client see and shape their event before a single crate ships. It is one system with four faces:

SurfaceWho uses itWhat it does
Sign-in pageEveryone/login.html. The front door — email + password, or an emailed one-time link.
DashboardOur team + clients/home.html. Where you land after signing in: every plan you're allowed to see as a card, new-plan button, client links, and each plan's photo library.
The StudioOur team + clientsThe editor itself. Draw the hall, lay out booths, place rentals, run safety checks, export paperwork.
3D previewEveryoneThe 3D button in the top bar. The live plan, stood up as a model you can orbit — drape at real heights, furniture at true size.
Exhibitor portalExhibitors/portal.html. An exhibitor signs in with an emailed link and manages just their booth.
Public directoryAttendees/viewer.html. A read-only, searchable floor plan you publish when the show is ready.

Everything runs from the same live database. When anyone moves a table, every other view — including the client's — is looking at the new truth.

2Signing in, and your dashboard

  1. Go to /login.html and enter your email and password — or click Email me a one-time sign-in link for a passwordless login.
  2. You land on the dashboard: every plan you're allowed to see, as cards. The first visit shows a short getting-started panel; reopen it any time with the Getting started button up top.
  3. Each card shows the booth count and hall size, plus (for staff) whether it's internal or assigned to a client. From a card you can Open plan in the Studio, view its Photos, assign it to a client, or generate a Client link.
  4. New plan (staff) creates a fresh hall and drops you straight into the Studio. The brand logo in the Studio's top-left always brings you back to the dashboard.

Access is decided by your role, and the database — not the app — enforces it:

3Finding your way around the Studio

Top bar

  • Plan name — click it and type to rename. Saved / Unsaved shows sync state beside it.
  • Undo / redo, zoom, fit — or Ctrl Z, scroll wheel, and F.
  • Grid · Snap · Labels — three toggles (G / S / L). Leave snap on; everything lands on clean 5 ft increments.
  • Local / your name — sign-in and cloud sync. Plans — open, create, import. Export — every output format. 3D — the walkthrough. Then theme and help.

Left panel — what you can place

  • Tool rail (icon strip): select, pan, booth, wall, dead space, fire exit, aisle, text, measure, fill-area.
  • Quick booths: one click arms a standard footprint (10×10, 10×20, 20×20…), then drag it onto the plan. It stays armed so you can place a whole run without re-clicking.
  • Catalog: every element type, grouped — booth spaces, structure, life safety, zones, amenities, booth contents, Source One rentals, utilities, electrical, annotation. The small colored badge on a row is a live count of how many are on this plan.
  • Bulk tools: generate whole booth rows/blocks, auto-number booths, and Import reference image… — the photo-to-plan workflow (section 4).

Right panel — information about things

  • Properties — whatever is selected: position, size, rotation, colors, and the fields that matter for that thing (booth number, drape height, table throw color, breaker size…).
  • Booths — the sellable inventory as a list: numbers, sizes, statuses, exhibitor assignments.
  • Safety — the rules engine's findings (section 9). Red items are errors; amber are warnings. Click one to jump to it.
  • Power — panels, distros, circuits, and per-booth load.
  • Drape — the pipe-and-drape takeoff, computed per run.
  • Plan — hall size, grid, dates (including the freeze date — section 8), and the reference-image controls.

Double-click any booth to step inside it and furnish just that footprint; the scope bar at the top of the canvas takes you back out.

4From venue photos to a working floor plan

This is the core field workflow: walk the venue, shoot it, and trace it into a clean digital plan the same day.

What to photograph on site

  • Best source: the posted floor plan. Every hall has a framed floor plan or fire-evacuation placard on the wall. Photograph that, not the room.
  • Straight on, square in frame. Stand directly in front of it. If the photo looks like a parallelogram, the trace will be skewed — retake it.
  • Whole sheet, no glare. Step back until the full sheet is in frame; angle slightly if lights reflect.
  • Record one real measurement. You will need a known length to scale the image. A standard double door is 6 ft; a column bay is usually a round number; or pace out one wall and write it down.
  • No placard? Shoot the whole floor from a balcony or mezzanine, as high and centred as possible.
💡
PDFs work too. If the venue emails a PDF plan, screenshot the page and import the screenshot.

Turning the photo into a plan

  1. In the Studio, open Plans → New plan and set the hall's rough width and height (you can correct it later).
  2. Click Import reference image… in the left panel. Read the capture checklist, click Choose photo…, and pick your picture. It drops onto the canvas at 50% opacity.
  3. Calibrate the scale. The app immediately asks you to drag across something whose real length you know — that double door, a printed dimension, a wall you paced. Drag it, type the real length, Apply scale. The image resizes and locks. Everything you trace from now on comes out at true dimensions.
  4. Trace the structure. Use the Wall tool (W) along the perimeter and partitions, then place columns, doors, stairs, loading docks, and fire exits from the Structure and Life-safety groups right on top of their photo counterparts.
  5. Mark what can't be sold. Dead space (D), aisles (A), and keep-clear fire lanes.
  6. Remove the photo. Right panel → Plan tab → Remove image. The photo was scaffolding; what remains is a clean, white, fully editable plan.
📷
Where the photos go: every photo you import into a cloud plan is automatically archived to that plan's photo library — open it from the plan's card on the dashboard (Photos). The original shot survives even after you remove the underlay, and you can upload extra site photos there directly.

Or: scan the room with an iPhone (LiDAR)

On a LiDAR-equipped iPhone (12 Pro or any newer Pro/Pro Max), you can skip the photo-and-trace workflow entirely:

  1. Install any room scanner app built on Apple's RoomPlan (search the App Store for "RoomPlan scanner" — several are free). Walk the room following the app's on-screen guide.
  2. Export the scan as JSON and get it to your Mac (AirDrop is fastest).
  3. In the Studio: Import reference image… → Import LiDAR scan (.json)…. Walls, doors, and recognised furniture are rebuilt as real, editable plan elements at their true measured dimensions — no tracing, no scale calibration.
ℹ️
Why an app is needed: Apple only exposes the LiDAR sensor to native apps, never to web pages — no website can read it directly. The scanner app does the capture; Floorplan Studio turns its export into your working plan.
💡
The Schaumburg Convention Center plan in your cloud account was built exactly this way — from a photographed paper plan to a 48-booth interactive layout. Open it to see the finished result.

Then lay in the booths

  1. Use Quick booths for one-offs, Generate booth row / block… for whole runs, or the fill-area tool (U) to flood a dragged region with booths on the standard 30 ft module (booth–aisle–booth).
  2. Run Auto-number booths — it numbers by aisle the way the industry reads them (hundreds digit = aisle, odd/even split per side).
  3. Watch the Safety tab as you go; it re-checks every edit (section 9).

5The Source One rentals catalog

Everything we rent is in the catalog under Source One rentals, drawn to its real footprint, so a floor plan doubles as an order sheet:

  • Tables — 6 ft and 8 ft banquet, 60″ rounds, 30″ cocktail high-tops, charging tables, coffee tables. Tables carry a Table throw color (black, royal blue, white, red, custom print) in Properties.
  • Seating & lounge — sofas, lounge chairs, branded cubes, bar stools.
  • Service pieces — bars, registration counters, podiums, display cases, kiosks/workstations.
  • Staging & structure — 4×8 stage decks (16/24/32 in heights), towers, entrance units, custom hardwall rooms, grid/slat wall.
  • Signage & graphics — banner stands, poster boards, LED posters, hanging & circle signs.
  • Flooring — carpet regions in any color.

Click an item to arm it, then click or drag on the plan. It stays armed for repeat placement — Esc or V returns to select. The badge next to each catalog row counts how many are already on the plan, and the Export menu turns the layout into paperwork: booth manifest, electrical schedule, drape order sheet, and per-booth work orders, all CSV.

6The 3D preview

  1. Click 3D in the top bar. The plan stands up into a model: drape rises to its actual ordered height, tables and sofas at true size, columns, stages, booth tiles colored by status with floating booth numbers.
  2. Drag to orbit · scroll to zoom · right-drag to pan.
  3. It stays live — leave 3D open while someone (you or the client) edits, and the model updates with every change.
  4. Back to plan returns to the 2D editor.
💡
This is the closer. When a client opens their plan and flips to 3D, they're standing in their event before we've loaded a truck. Encourage them to use it — it surfaces "actually, move the bar" conversations weeks early, while moving the bar is free.

7How multi-tenant works

Every client company is a tenant — a sealed workspace inside the same system.

  • Each plan (show) can be assigned to exactly one client. Unassigned plans are internal and invisible to every client.
  • A client login belongs to one company. When that person signs in, the system behaves as if their plans are the only plans that exist: their Plans list, their searches, their exports — all filtered to their company.
  • Both sides edit the same plan. You and the client work on one live layout — there are no copies to reconcile. Their edits appear for you instantly, and vice versa.
  • After a show's freeze date (set in the Plan tab), client editing locks automatically — what's on the plan is what gets built. Staff can still edit.
🔒
Isolation is enforced by the database, not the interface. Row-level security in Postgres decides what every request may touch, using the account's identity — so even someone bypassing the app entirely and talking to the API directly gets the same walls. Client A can never see, list, or modify anything of Client B's. This was verified with live cross-tenant attack tests before launch.

8The Admin panel — clients, logins & one-time links

Where it is: the Admin button on the dashboard — or, inside the Studio, click your name in the top-right (the cloud button), then Team & access…. The panel has two tabs: Clients and Team & access. You need an Admin or Planner account to see it.

⚡
Shortcut: the Client link button on any assigned plan's dashboard card generates the same one-time link without opening the Admin panel.

Setting up a new client, start to finish

  1. Add the company. Clients tab → type the company name (contact email optional) → Add client.
  2. Give them a plan. Click the company to expand it. Under Plans, pick a plan from the dropdown and Assign plan. (Build the plan first — section 4 — or assign it later; you can also Unassign any time.)
  3. Copy their link. Under Client access, every company has one permanent link — unique to them, never expires. Copy link.
  4. Set their password. Type a password you choose (8+ characters) → Set password. That's what activates the link; change it here any time.
  5. Send them both. Link + password, however you like — email, text, Teams. They open their link, see their company's name, enter the password, and land in their workspace. Same link, same password, every visit.

How the client link works

  • The link is permanent and unique to that company — it opens a sign-in page for them alone (no email to type, just their password).
  • The password is the lock: change it in the panel and the old one stops working immediately — the link itself never changes.
  • Every password you set is written to the audit log with your name on it.
⚠️
Link + password together are the key. Send them directly to the client, ideally in separate messages, and never post either anywhere public. If a password leaks, change it in the panel — one click, and the leak is dead while the client's link keeps working with the new password.

Team & access tab — your internal staff

  • Add a team member (admins only): name, email, password, and role — the account works immediately, no invitations or confirmation emails.
  • Every internal account is listed with a role dropdown and an Active toggle. The system refuses to remove the last active admin, so you can't lock yourself out.

9What the client sees, and the safety net under everyone

The client's experience

  • They open your link and land in the same Studio you use — but containing only their company's plans.
  • They can move furniture, place rentals from the catalog, add notes, and flip to 3D to walk their event.
  • They cannot see other clients, other plans, team accounts, or the Admin panel — those don't exist in their world.
  • On the freeze date their plan becomes read-only for them, so late-night surprises can't reach the build.

The rules engine (the Safety tab)

Every edit — yours or the client's — is re-checked against the house rules:

  • Errors (red): overlapping booths, anything outside the hall, booths on dead space or obstructions, blocked fire exits / egress clearance (10 ft default), missing exits.
  • Warnings (amber): narrow aisles, height-limit breaches by space type, circuits over capacity, panels loaded past 80% of the breaker (NEC 210.19), booths out of reach of an electrical bus, missing or duplicate booth numbers, voltage drop.

So even with a client dragging things around unsupervised, the plan tells you — loudly and specifically — the moment anything becomes unbuildable or unsafe.

10Exhibitor portal & public directory

  • Exhibitor portal (/portal.html): exhibitors sign in with an emailed link, see their booth on the real floor plan, submit their details, and order services. Link an exhibitor to a booth from the Booths tab, and their email address becomes their key.
  • Public directory (/viewer.html): when a show is ready, use Export → Publish public directory…. Attendees get a searchable, read-only floor plan — with the same 3D preview — and nothing else.

11Saving, cloud & exports

  • Signed out, plans save to the browser you're using (the button reads Local). Fine for sketching; invisible to everyone else.
  • Signed in, plans live in the cloud: every device, every teammate (per role), every client (per assignment) sees the same current version. Use the cloud panel's upload action to move a local sketch to the cloud.
  • Exports (top bar): PNG / SVG / print-PDF of the drawing; plan file (.json) for backup or transfer; and the ops paperwork — booth manifest, electrical schedule, drape order sheet, booth work orders.

13Troubleshooting

SymptomFix
Client says their link doesn't workIt was already used or expired — generate a fresh one from the Admin panel. Or just have them sign in with email + password.
Client can't see their planThe plan isn't assigned to their company. Admin panel → their company → Assign plan.
Client can see but not editThe show is past its freeze date (Plan tab), or their account was deactivated.
